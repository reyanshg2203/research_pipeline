A contractor is designing a roof truss for a house and needs to understand the internal geometry of the structure. She has a right triangle PQR where the two legs meeting at the right angle Q measure PQ = 9 feet and QR = 12 feet, and the hypotenuse PR = 15 feet.

To reinforce the truss, she plans to install a vertical support post QS that runs perpendicular from the right angle Q straight down to the hypotenuse PR, meeting it at point D. This altitude divides the hypotenuse into two segments: PS and SR.

The contractor recalls from her geometry studies that when an altitude is drawn to the hypotenuse of a right triangle, the altitude itself is the geometric mean of the two segments it creates on the hypotenuse. In other words, QS is the geometric mean of PS and SR.

Using this relationship, how long should the support post QS be?
