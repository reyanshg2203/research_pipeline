A surveyor is measuring two triangular plots of land to determine if they share similar characteristics. The first plot, triangle ABC, has a side AB = 10 meters and two of its angles measure ∠A = 52° and ∠B = 68°. The second plot, triangle DEF, has a side DE = 15 meters and two of its angles measure ∠D = 52° and ∠F = 60°.

The surveyor notes that since the interior angles of any triangle must sum to 180°, she can find the third angle in each triangle. She also observes that the two plots are similar by the Angle-Angle Triangle Similarity Theorem, with a scale factor of 1.5 relating them — specifically, because the angle pairs ∠A ≅ ∠D and ∠B ≅ ∠E correspond between the triangles.

What is the measure of ∠E in triangle DEF?
