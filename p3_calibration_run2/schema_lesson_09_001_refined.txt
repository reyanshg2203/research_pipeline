A surveyor is mapping two triangular plots of land. The first plot, triangle ABC, has a side AB = 10 meters and two known angles: ∠A = 52° and ∠B = 68°. The second plot, triangle DEF, has a side DE = 15 meters with two known angles: ∠D = 52° and ∠F = 60°.

Since the interior angles of any triangle must sum to 180°, you can find the third angle in each triangle. In triangle ABC, the three angles ∠A, ∠B, and ∠C must add up to 180°. Similarly, in triangle DEF, the angles ∠D, ∠E, and ∠F must sum to 180°.

The surveyor notices that ∠A ≅ ∠D (both 52°) and ∠B ≅ ∠E. Because two pairs of corresponding angles are congruent, triangle ABC is similar to triangle DEF by the Angle-Angle Triangle Similarity Theorem, with a scale factor of 1.5 (the ratio DE : AB = 15 : 10).
