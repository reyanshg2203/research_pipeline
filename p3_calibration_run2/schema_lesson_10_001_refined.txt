A surveyor is comparing two triangular plots of land. The first plot, triangle PQR, has sides measuring PQ = 9 meters, QR = 12 meters, and PR = 15 meters. She discovers a second, smaller plot, triangle XYZ, with two of its sides already measured: XY = 6 meters and YZ = 8 meters.

By checking the ratios of the corresponding sides, she confirms that triangle XYZ is similar to triangle PQR by the Side-Side-Side Similarity Theorem, with a scale factor of 1.5 (meaning each side of PQR is 1.5 times the length of the corresponding side in XYZ).

To complete her survey map, she needs to find the length of the third side XZ.
