A contractor is designing two similar roof trusses for a pair of buildings. The first truss, triangle PQR, is a right triangle with legs PQ = 9 feet and QR = 12 feet, meeting at the right angle Q. The second truss, triangle XYZ, is also a right triangle with a right angle at Y, and one of its legs measures XY = 6 feet.

The contractor knows that the two trusses are similar by AA similarity — specifically, the angles at Q and Y are congruent (both right angles), and the angles at P and X are also congruent. Because the trusses have the same shape, the contractor can use the proportional relationships between corresponding sides to find the unknown dimensions.

What is the length of side YZ in the smaller truss XYZ?
